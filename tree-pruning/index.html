Pruning can need council approval too

Councils limit how much of a protected tree can be pruned without a permit. The limits and conditions differ between councils, so check yours before work starts.

  • Northern Beaches Council’s page says pruning of up to 10% of the canopy within 12 months can be done without a permit, in line with the Australian Standard for pruning amenity trees (AS 4373).
  • Hornsby Shire Council’s page lists limited pruning of live branches that are causing a nuisance, such as touching the roof, up to 10% of the leaf canopy, as exempt. Larger pruning needs a permit.
  • Ku-ring-gai Council says it does not generally permit “topping” to reduce a tree’s height.

These exemptions don’t apply everywhere. Heritage properties, heritage conservation areas and threatened ecological communities have stricter rules, and the NSW Planning policy changes page describes 2026 changes for dead and dying trees that some council pages don’t reflect. These are summaries with conditions, not permission, so confirm with your council before work starts.

If the branches belong to a neighbour’s tree, Ku-ring-gai Council advises that you need the tree owner’s permission before pruning. For disputes, see the Land and Environment Court’s trees and hedges information.

What to include in your enquiry

  • Which branches you want pruned and why: for example touching the roof, overhanging a driveway or blocking light.
  • Roughly how much you want taken off, and the tree’s approximate height.
  • Whether any branches are near power lines, because work near them has extra safety requirements.
  • Access to the tree, and whether you have council approval or are still checking.

Photos that help

Photos are optional, but they help the contractor understand the job when they review your enquiry. Useful photos show:

  • the whole tree, taken from far enough back to see its full height
  • the base of the trunk and any exposed roots
  • what is around it, such as the house, fences, a pool, power lines or a driveway
  • the path a contractor would use to reach it, including gates and steps
  • any damage you are worried about, such as split or dead branches.

You can attach up to 5 photos in JPEG, PNG, WebP or HEIC format, each up to 4 MB and 12 MB in total. Large photos are resized in your browser before sending, which also removes most embedded information such as location data. Please don’t include photos of people.

What happens after you send your enquiry

  1. We receive your enquiry by email and review it. This is done manually, so it isn’t instant.
  2. We forward your details and any photos to a tree contractor for review, as you agree to when you tick the consent box.
  3. They may contact you to discuss the job by your preferred method: email, or phone if you choose it and include your number.
  4. If they can take on the job, they assess it, explain what approvals may apply and provide a quote.
  5. You decide whether to go ahead. Any work, timing and payment are arranged directly with the contractor.

Sending an enquiry is free. It is not a booking, a contract or approval to remove or prune a tree. Sydney Tree Connect does not carry out tree work.

Frequently asked questions

How much can I prune without a council permit?

It depends on your council and the tree. Northern Beaches and Hornsby councils describe limits of up to 10% of the canopy, each with conditions, and some trees have no exemptions at all. Ask your council before work starts. See our council tree rules guides.

Is tree lopping the same as pruning?

People often say “lopping” for any tree cutting, but councils treat it differently. Ku-ring-gai Council says it does not generally permit topping, and Northern Beaches Council says it won’t permit lopping or topping, or pruning that doesn’t meet the Australian Standard. Ask the contractor what pruning they propose.

Can I prune branches that hang over from my neighbour’s tree?

Ku-ring-gai Council advises that you need the tree owner’s permission before pruning their tree, and council rules can still apply. Check with your council, and in a dispute see the Land and Environment Court information on trees and hedges.

Questions to ask the contractor

  • Will the pruning follow the Australian Standard for pruning amenity trees (AS 4373)?
  • Is the amount of pruning within what my council allows without a permit, or do I need approval?
  • What does the written quote include, such as green waste removal and clean-up?
