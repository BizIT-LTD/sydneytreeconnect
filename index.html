Before removing or pruning a tree

Tree rules in NSW differ from council to council, and fines can apply for work done without the right approval. This is a general checklist, not legal advice or a decision about your tree.

  1. Confirm ownership or authority and identify the treeMake sure you own the property or are authorised to act for the owner, and be clear about exactly which tree is involved.
  2. Identify your local council and check its current tree rulesEach council’s development control plan sets out which trees are protected. Check the current version on the council’s website or contact them.
  3. Check which approvals applyDepending on the tree and the land, you may need a council permit, development consent, an arborist report or another approval. Heritage items and areas can need development consent.
  4. Get approval, or written evidence of an exemptionObtain any required approval before work starts. If you believe the work is exempt, keep documented evidence of the exemption.
  5. Have the contractor check before work startsThe contractor should verify your documents, the tree’s identity and any approval conditions before doing any work.

A few common misunderstandings

Owning the property, a tree being dead or dying, a tree seeming dangerous or a tree being a certain size does not automatically mean it can be removed. NSW rules updated in 2026 say protected dead and dying trees now need a permit or approval, and the safety-risk exemption depends on your council agreeing the tree poses an imminent risk. Street trees and other trees on public land need approval from the relevant authority. A neighbour’s tree needs your neighbour’s consent or, in a dispute, the Land and Environment Court process. Please don’t attempt to fell a tree yourself.

Frequently asked questions

Are you the tree company?

No. Sydney Tree Connect is an enquiry and referral website. We receive your enquiry and pass it on to a tree contractor. The contractor assesses the job, checks what permissions are needed, provides a quote and carries out any work you agree to.

Who will contact me, and how?

The tree contractor we pass your enquiry to will contact you. They will use your preferred contact method. If you choose phone, you need to include your phone number so they can call you. Enquiries are forwarded manually, so it isn’t instant.

Do I need to send photos?

No, photos are optional. A few clear photos of the whole tree, the area around it and any damaged branches can help the contractor understand the job before they contact you. You can attach up to 5 photos. Please don’t include photos of people.

I’m not sure whether I need council approval. Can I still enquire?

Yes. Choose “Not sure” in the form. The contractor can talk through what may apply, but your council makes the decision about permits and exemptions. Submitting an enquiry is not an approval to remove or prune a tree. See the checklist above for official sources.

What if I don’t own the property?

You can still ask for general guidance, for example if you are a tenant or a neighbour. Work can only go ahead with the authority of the property owner and any required approvals. Trees on a neighbour’s land or on the street need the neighbour’s consent or approval from the relevant authority.

Who does the work, and who do I pay?

The contractor does the work, and you pay the contractor directly under the terms they give you. Sydney Tree Connect doesn’t take payments from customers. Check the contractor’s quote, terms, licences and insurance with them before you agree to any work.

Is this form for emergencies?

No. This form is not monitored for emergencies. If a tree has fallen on a building or power lines, or anyone is in danger, call Triple Zero (000). For storm and flood help, contact the NSW SES on 132 500. Keep well away from fallen power lines.

Does submitting an enquiry book any work?

No. An enquiry is not a booking or a contract. You decide whether to accept the contractor’s quote.
