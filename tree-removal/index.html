Check approval before a tree is removed

In most of Sydney you need your council’s permission to remove a tree, even on your own property. Each council’s development control plan sets out which trees are protected and which work is exempt.

  • The NSW Planning policy changes page (updated 23 October 2026) says protected dead and dying trees need a council permit or Native Vegetation Panel approval, and that the safety-risk exemption applies only where the council agrees the risk is imminent. Some council pages still describe different exemptions, so ask your council which rule applies before any work.
  • Heritage items, heritage conservation areas and threatened ecological communities usually have stricter rules.
  • Street trees need approval from the council. A tree on a neighbour’s land needs your neighbour’s consent or, in a dispute, the Land and Environment Court process.

Our council tree rules guides summarise the official information for Ku-ring-gai, Hornsby and Northern Beaches. If you’re not sure whether you need approval, you can still enquire and choose “Not sure”.

What to include in your enquiry

  • The type of tree if you know it, its rough height and how thick the trunk is.
  • Where it is: front or back yard, and how close it is to the house, fences, a pool or power lines.
  • Access: side gate width, slopes, steps and whether a vehicle can get close.
  • Why you want it removed, and whether you have council approval or are still checking.
  • Whether you would also like the stump ground down. Mention it in the job description.

Photos that help

Photos are optional, but they help the contractor understand the job when they review your enquiry. Useful photos show:

  • the whole tree, taken from far enough back to see its full height
  • the base of the trunk and any exposed roots
  • what is around it, such as the house, fences, a pool, power lines or a driveway
  • the path a contractor would use to reach it, including gates and steps
  • any damage you are worried about, such as split or dead branches.

You can attach up to 5 photos in JPEG, PNG, WebP or HEIC format, each up to 4 MB and 12 MB in total. Large photos are resized in your browser before sending, which also removes most embedded information such as location data. Please don’t include photos of people.

What happens after you send your enquiry

  1. We receive your enquiry by email and review it. This is done manually, so it isn’t instant.
  2. We forward your details and any photos to a tree contractor for review, as you agree to when you tick the consent box.
  3. They may contact you to discuss the job by your preferred method: email, or phone if you choose it and include your number.
  4. If they can take on the job, they assess it, explain what approvals may apply and provide a quote.
  5. You decide whether to go ahead. Any work, timing and payment are arranged directly with the contractor.

Sending an enquiry is free. It is not a booking, a contract or approval to remove or prune a tree. Sydney Tree Connect does not carry out tree work.

What affects the price

Every job is different, so the contractor quotes after assessing the tree. Size, access, nearby structures and power lines, the tree’s condition, green waste and any council requirements all make a difference. Read our tree removal cost guide for what to look for in a quote.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need council approval to remove a tree on my own property?

Often, yes. Council rules decide which trees are protected, even on your own land, and exemptions have conditions. NSW and council guidance currently differ for dead and dangerous trees, so ask your council which rule applies before any work. If you’re not sure, you can still enquire and choose “Not sure”. See our council tree rules guides.

Is the stump removed as part of tree removal?

Not always. Stump grinding may be quoted separately. If you want the stump ground, mention it in your job description and check whether the written quote includes it. See stump grinding enquiries.

Can I enquire about a tree on my neighbour’s property?

You can ask for general guidance, but work can only go ahead with the tree owner’s consent and any required approvals. In a dispute about a neighbour’s tree, see the Land and Environment Court information on trees and hedges.

Questions to ask the contractor

  • What does the written quote include: removal to ground level, stump grinding, green waste removal and clean-up?
  • Can you show me your insurance and any qualifications that matter for this job?
  • Who checks that the council approval or exemption covers this work, and how will you meet any conditions?
