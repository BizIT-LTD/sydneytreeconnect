Hornsby Shire Council tree rules

Last reviewed: 4 November 2026, against Hornsby Shire Council’s trees on private property page and NSW Government sources.

A summary of what Hornsby Shire Council says about removing and pruning trees on private property. It is general information, not legal advice or a decision about your tree. Always check the current rules with the council before work starts.

When you need a permit

Council says that if you want to prune or remove trees on private property, you will generally need a permit. Permits are assessed by one of Council’s arborists. Council lists these situations as needing a permit:

  • tree work that is not addressing a nuisance
  • removing or pruning more than 10% of a tree
  • trees in a heritage conservation area, heritage listed, or subject to heritage conditions
  • trees on land mapped as Biodiversity on the Hornsby LEP Terrestrial Biodiversity Map
  • trees that are home to threatened species, or in habitat for threatened species, populations or ecological communities
  • trees, alive or dead, in an endangered or critically endangered ecological community
  • properties where a development consent requires trees to be kept or replanted, or where removal needs a development application.

Council lists heritage trees as needing a permit, but the NSW Government says clearing that affects a heritage-listed tree, or is in a heritage area, needs a development application. Ask Council which approval applies before work starts.

Exemptions Council lists

Council says these exemptions do not apply in a heritage conservation area, a locally significant ecological community, or an endangered or critically endangered ecological community, including its 50 metre buffer. Elsewhere, Council lists work including the items below. Each exemption has conditions set out on Council’s page. This summary is not permission, so confirm with Council before work starts.

  • removing deadwood for tree maintenance, in line with the Australian Standard for pruning amenity trees (AS 4373)
  • pruning live branches only where they are causing a nuisance, such as touching the roof, up to 10% of the leaf canopy
  • removing or pruning a tree whose trunk base is within 3 metres of the foundation of an approved building, not including detached garages, carports and other ancillary buildings
  • removing a tree under 3 metres tall that is not in native vegetation
  • trees on Council’s exempt species list
  • clearing under the NSW Rural Fire Service 10/50 code on properties it applies to.

Council’s tree prune or removal decision tool can help you work out which rules apply.

NSW and council guidance differ. An exemption listed here is not permission. The NSW Planning policy changes page (updated 23 October 2026) says protected dead and dying trees need a council permit or Native Vegetation Panel approval, and that the safety-risk exemption applies only where the council agrees the tree poses an imminent safety risk. We couldn’t confirm from official sources when these changes took effect or exactly which work they cover, and some council pages still describe different dead-tree and risk exemptions. We don’t say which rule applies to your tree. Ask the council before any work starts, and get its answer in writing.

Hornsby’s page, checked on 4 November 2026, describes an exemption for a dead tree that is not habitat for native wildlife, or a tree that is an imminent risk to life or property, supported by photos, video or an arborist’s statement. This differs from the NSW Planning guidance above. Don’t treat either as permission: ask Council which rule applies to your tree, and get its answer in writing before any work.

How to apply

  • Apply and pay through Council’s online services portal, or use the application form on Council’s trees on private property page.
  • The property owner must complete the consent section. Council says tenants cannot give permission, and strata, company title and shared property rules also apply.
  • An arborist report prepared to Council’s guidelines may be required. For trees in poor condition or causing structural damage, Council may ask for an AQF Level 5 arborist report or a structural engineer’s assessment.
  • Council’s page doesn’t list current fees or processing times. Check them with Council when you apply.

Replacement planting

Council says approved removals must be replaced with a tree of similar habit that is indigenous to the area, planted as near as practicable, grown to maturity and replaced if it fails, under its Green Offsets Code.

Neighbours’ trees

See Council’s trees on neighbouring property page. Disputes can go to the Land and Environment Court.
