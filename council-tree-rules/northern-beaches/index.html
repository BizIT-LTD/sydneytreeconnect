Northern Beaches Council tree rules

Last reviewed: 4 November 2026, against Northern Beaches Council’s removing and pruning trees on private land page and NSW Government sources.

A summary of what Northern Beaches Council says about removing and pruning trees on private property. It is general information, not legal advice or a decision about your tree. Always check the current rules with the council before work starts.

Exemptions Council lists

Council’s page lists these exemptions, among others. Each has conditions set out on Council’s page. This summary is not permission, so confirm with Council before work starts. Council says a tree can be removed without a permit if it:

  • is under 5 metres tall
  • is on Council’s exempt tree species list, unless it is a heritage item or in a heritage area
  • is within 2 metres of an existing approved building, measured from the building to the base of the trunk. Decks, pergolas, sheds and patios don’t count as buildings for this rule.
  • is part of a hedge (two or more trees planted to form a hedge, at least 2.5 metres tall), unless a development consent requires it to be kept
  • is a priority weed listed in the Greater Sydney Regional Weed Management Plan.

Pruning without a permit is limited to up to 10% of the canopy within 12 calendar months, in line with the Australian Standard for pruning amenity trees (AS 4373), plus removing deadwood and parasitic plants such as mistletoe.

NSW and council guidance differ. An exemption listed here is not permission. The NSW Planning policy changes page (updated 23 October 2026) says protected dead and dying trees need a council permit or Native Vegetation Panel approval, and that the safety-risk exemption applies only where the council agrees the tree poses an imminent safety risk. We couldn’t confirm from official sources when these changes took effect or exactly which work they cover, and some council pages still describe different dead-tree and risk exemptions. We don’t say which rule applies to your tree. Ask the council before any work starts, and get its answer in writing.

Northern Beaches’ page, checked on 4 November 2026, lists exemptions for dead trees that are not habitat, storm-damaged trees that remain dangerous, and trees certified as an imminent danger by an AQF Level 5 arborist. These differ from the NSW Planning guidance above. Don’t treat either as permission: ask Council which rule applies to your tree, and get its answer in writing before any work.

When you need approval

Council’s page says approval is needed for:

  • Native vegetation clearing covered by Council’s development control plan.
  • Threatened species, their habitat, or endangered ecological communities.
  • Heritage items and trees in a heritage conservation area. You can check this with Council’s planning rules property search.
  • Trees that a development consent requires to be kept.

For heritage items and heritage areas, the NSW Government says clearing needs a development application. Ask Council which approval applies before work starts.

Properties on the NSW Biodiversity Values Map may need approval from the Native Vegetation Panel rather than Council, particularly for healthy native trees that are not part of a development application.

What Council says it will and won’t approve

Council may permit removal of significant trees with a qualified arborist’s report, hazardous trees, and trees conflicting with buildings once engineering alternatives have been considered. It says it will not permit, among other things:

  • removal of healthy, stable trees, or removal for views
  • removal for solar access, leaf, fruit or sap drop, or bird or bat droppings
  • removal for allergies unless medically proven, as set out on Council’s page
  • lopping or topping, or pruning that doesn’t meet the Australian Standard
  • removal for a complying development certificate, or applications without the owner’s signature.

How to apply

  • Check the exemptions first.
  • Apply online with Council’s tree removal and pruning application, uploading a completed tree removal site plan with the owner’s consent. A paper form can be lodged at a customer service centre.
  • Council lists its fees on its fees and charges page, including a fee if you want to attend Council’s inspection.
  • Permits expire 12 months after they are issued, must be displayed at the front of the property during the work, and can include replanting conditions.
