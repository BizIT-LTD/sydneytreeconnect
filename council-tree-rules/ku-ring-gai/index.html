Ku-ring-gai Council tree rules

Last reviewed: 4 November 2026, against Ku-ring-gai Council’s trees on your property page and NSW Government sources.

A summary of what Ku-ring-gai Council says about removing and pruning trees on private property. It is general information, not legal advice or a decision about your tree. Always check the current rules with the council before work starts.

When you need permission

Ku-ring-gai Council defines a tree as a perennial plant with a self-supporting woody or fibrous stem, native or exotic, that is 5 metres or more in height, or that has a trunk diameter of 150 mm or more measured at ground level. Work on these trees needs a tree application unless an exemption applies. The exemptions are set out in Part 13 of the Ku-ring-gai Development Control Plan (Tree and Vegetation Preservation), which is linked from Council’s trees on your property page.

Council says no exemptions apply to trees on a heritage item property, in a heritage conservation area, in a threatened ecological community or that are a listed threatened species.

Council also says it does not generally permit “topping” to reduce a tree’s height.

Dead, dying and hazardous trees

  • Dead trees are not automatically exempt. Council says no work can be carried out on a dead tree until it has confirmed in writing that the tree is dead and not needed as habitat for native animals. Email photos and your property address to krg@krg.nsw.gov.au.
  • For a tree you think is hazardous, Council says to contact it for approval before any work, and to have the tree assessed by a consulting arborist first.

How to apply

  • Apply online using Council’s tree application form.
  • Mandatory documents: the reasons for tree pruning or removal form, a property site map, photos and the owner’s consent form. Save each as a PDF under 4 MB.
  • An arborist report is not required to apply. If you include one, it must follow Council’s guidelines for arborist reports.
  • Council requests payment by emailed invoice after it accepts the application. Fees are not refunded if permission isn’t granted. On 4 November 2026 the page listed $88.00 for a basic tree removal application (one tree or hedge), $44.00 for pruning and $13.00 for each additional tree or hedge, with lower pensioner rates in some cases.
  • Council’s FAQs say processing takes about 6 to 8 weeks from when the application is acknowledged in writing, and longer when volumes are high. Permits are valid for 12 months from the date of issue.

Tree work connected with a development application, complying development or a new driveway is not dealt with through a tree application. Council suggests booking a Duty Planner appointment for those.

Bushfire 10/50 rules

Some Ku-ring-gai properties can prune or remove trees under the NSW Rural Fire Service 10/50 code. Council says the code only applies to trees whose trunk is within 10 metres of certain walls of the home, and not to every tree on an eligible property. Use the RFS 10/50 information and eligibility tool and Council’s bushfire protection rules for trees. Trees outside the code need a tree application.

Neighbours’ trees

Council says it cannot intervene in tree issues between neighbours, and that you need the tree owner’s permission before pruning their tree. Disputes can go to the Land and Environment Court.

NSW and council guidance differ. An exemption listed here is not permission. The NSW Planning policy changes page (updated 23 October 2026) says protected dead and dying trees need a council permit or Native Vegetation Panel approval, and that the safety-risk exemption applies only where the council agrees the tree poses an imminent safety risk. We couldn’t confirm from official sources when these changes took effect or exactly which work they cover, and some council pages still describe different dead-tree and risk exemptions. We don’t say which rule applies to your tree. Ask the council before any work starts, and get its answer in writing.
