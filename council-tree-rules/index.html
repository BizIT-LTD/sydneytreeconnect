Council tree rules in Sydney

Each council sets its own rules about which trees need permission to remove or prune. These guides summarise the official information and link to the source pages. They are general information, not legal advice or a decision about your tree.

Last reviewed: 4 November 2026

Rules that apply across Sydney

  • Council permission is usually needed. The NSW Government says you generally need permission from your council to clear trees and vegetation, even on your own property. The rules come from Chapter 2 of the State Environmental Planning Policy (Biodiversity and Conservation) 2021 and each council’s development control plan.
  • Dead and dying trees. The NSW Planning policy changes page (updated 23 October 2026) says protected dead and dying trees need a council permit or Native Vegetation Panel approval, and that previous exemptions no longer apply. We couldn’t confirm when these changes took effect, and some council pages still describe different exemptions. Ask your council which rule applies, and get its answer in writing before any work.
  • Trees that seem dangerous. The same NSW page says the risk exemption applies only where the council agrees the tree poses an imminent safety risk. Council pages describe this differently, so ask your council before any work.
  • Heritage. Work affecting a heritage-listed tree or a tree in a heritage area generally needs a development application.
  • Larger clearing and the Biodiversity Values Map. Clearing above Biodiversity Offsets Scheme thresholds needs Native Vegetation Panel approval rather than a council permit.
  • Bushfire-prone land. Some properties can clear vegetation under the NSW Rural Fire Service 10/50 Vegetation Clearing Code of Practice. Check eligibility for your property and each tree.
  • Penalties. NSW Planning says penalty notices for illegal clearing start at $3,000 for an individual, or $6,000 for larger trees or significant vegetation, and court penalties can be much higher.
  • Neighbours and street trees. A tree on a neighbour’s land needs their consent, or the Land and Environment Court process in a dispute. Street trees are managed by the council.

Not sure which council covers your property?

Use the Office of Local Government’s find your local council tool. If your council isn’t listed here, check its website for “tree permit” or “tree application”, or contact it directly.
