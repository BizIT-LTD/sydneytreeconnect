What affects the cost of tree removal in Sydney

Tree removal prices vary a lot from job to job, so the only reliable price is a written quote from a contractor who has seen the tree. This guide explains what usually affects the price, so you can understand and compare quotes.

Last reviewed: 4 November 2026

Why we don’t list price ranges

We haven’t found Sydney tree removal price ranges from an independent, reliable source. The published figures we reviewed came from commercial and lead-generation websites and varied widely, so we don’t repeat them. The only prices on this page are official council fees, with their source.

The main cost factors

  • Size of the tree. Height, trunk thickness and canopy spread affect how long the job takes and how much material there is to remove.
  • Access. Narrow side gates, steep blocks, steps and limited parking can mean more work by hand, or that larger equipment can’t be used.
  • What is nearby. A tree close to the house, roof, fences, a pool or power lines may need to be taken down in smaller sections. Work near power lines has extra safety requirements.
  • The tree’s condition. A dead, decayed or storm-damaged tree can be less predictable and may need a more careful approach.
  • Green waste. Ask whether removing branches, logs and mulch from your property is included, or whether some is left on site.
  • The stump. Grinding the stump may be quoted separately. Ask whether it is included. See stump grinding enquiries.
  • Timing. If you need work done urgently, ask whether that changes the price.
  • Council requirements. Application fees, a report from an arborist if one is required, and permit conditions such as replacement planting can add to the overall cost.

Council fees and reports

Councils set their own application fees and update them, usually each financial year. As an example, on 4 November 2026 Ku-ring-gai Council’s trees on your property page listed a standard fee of $88.00 for a basic tree removal application (one tree or hedge), $44.00 for a pruning application and $13.00 for each additional tree or hedge, with lower pensioner rates in some cases. Ku-ring-gai says fees are not refunded if permission isn’t granted. Check your council’s current fees before applying.

Whether you need an arborist’s report also depends on the council and the tree. Ku-ring-gai says a report is not required to submit an application. Northern Beaches says removal of significant trees needs a report from a qualified arborist, and Hornsby may ask for one in some cases. See our council tree rules guides.

How to compare quotes

  • Get the quote in writing, with GST shown.
  • Check exactly what is included: removal to ground level, stump grinding, green waste removal and clean-up.
  • Ask who is responsible for checking that the council approval or exemption covers the work, and for meeting any permit conditions.
  • Ask to see the contractor’s insurance and any qualifications that matter to you.
  • Check the payment terms before you agree. Don’t start any work until the approval question is settled.
